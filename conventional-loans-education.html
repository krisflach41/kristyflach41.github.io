What is a Conventional Loan?

A conventional loan is a mortgage that is not insured or guaranteed by the federal government. Most conventional loans follow guidelines set by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, which are government-sponsored enterprises that buy mortgages from lenders. These are called "conforming loans" because they conform to the agencies' standards.

Benefits

  • Lower down payment options available (as low as 3%)
  • Competitive interest rates
  • PMI can be canceled once you reach 20% equity
  • Can be used for primary residence, second home, or investment property
  • No upfront mortgage insurance premium (unlike FHA)
  • Wide variety of loan terms available (10, 15, 20, 30 years)

Who Should Consider a Conventional Loan?

Conventional loans work best for borrowers who have:

  • Credit scores of 640 or higher (740+ for best rates)
  • Stable income and employment history (2 years)
  • At least 3-5% saved for down payment
  • Debt-to-income ratio under 45%
  • Plans to build equity and cancel PMI in the future

Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI)

If you put down less than 20%, you'll pay PMI, which protects the lender. The good news? PMI automatically cancels when you reach 20% equity through payments or home appreciation. Costs typically range from 0.5% to 1.5% of the loan amount annually.

Special Programs

HomeReady® (Fannie Mae): 3% down payment option for low-to-moderate income borrowers. Income limits apply.

Home Possible® (Freddie Mac): Similar to HomeReady with 3% down for income-qualified buyers.

Conventional vs. Other Loan Types

Conventional loans typically require stronger credit and more money down than FHA loans, but offer more flexibility and lower costs long-term. They're often the best choice for borrowers with good credit (680+) and at least 5% down payment.
