What is a USDA Loan?

USDA loans are zero down payment mortgages designed to promote homeownership in rural and suburban areas. These loans are guaranteed by the U.S. Department of Agriculture and offer competitive rates and low fees to low-to-moderate income borrowers.

Income Limits

📊 Important: Household Income Limits Apply

USDA loans are designed for low-to-moderate income buyers. Your total household income (including all adult household members, even if not on the loan) cannot exceed 115% of the area median income.

Income limits vary significantly by county and can range from $91,900 to over $160,000 in high-cost areas. Always verify the specific limit for your target area.

Property Eligibility

The property must be located in a USDA-eligible rural area:

  • Generally, areas with populations under 35,000
  • Many suburban communities qualify
  • Property must be your primary residence
  • Single-family homes only (1-4 units not permitted)
  • Must meet USDA health and safety standards

Note: Despite the "rural" designation, many suburban areas near cities qualify. The USDA provides an online eligibility map to check specific addresses.

USDA Guarantee Fee

USDA loans require a guarantee fee (similar to mortgage insurance):

  • Upfront Fee: 1.0% of loan amount (can be rolled into loan)
  • Annual Fee: 0.35% of loan balance, paid monthly
  • Duration: Life of the loan (cannot be canceled)

Note: USDA fees are lower than FHA mortgage insurance.

Major Benefits

  • Zero down payment (100% financing)
  • Low mortgage insurance costs (lower than FHA)
  • Competitive interest rates
  • Fixed-rate loans only (30-year terms)
  • Gift funds allowed for closing costs
  • Seller can contribute up to 6% toward closing costs
  • Flexible credit requirements

Who Should Consider a USDA Loan?

USDA loans work best for borrowers who:

  • Have little to no money saved for a down payment
  • Meet the income limits for their area
  • Are buying in an eligible rural or suburban location
  • Plan to use the home as their primary residence
  • Have credit scores of 640 or higher
  • Have stable employment history (2 years)

Additional Requirements

  • Debt-to-Income Ratio: Typically 41% or lower
  • Employment: Stable 2-year history required
  • Citizenship: U.S. citizen or permanent resident
  • Occupancy: Must move in within 60 days of closing
  • Credit: Clean credit history with no recent bankruptcies or foreclosures

USDA vs. Other Zero-Down Options

USDA vs. VA: If you're eligible for VA benefits, VA loans are usually better (no guarantee fee, no income limits, more property types allowed). USDA is great for non-military buyers in rural areas.

USDA vs. FHA: USDA requires no down payment and has lower fees than FHA, but requires eligible location and income limits. FHA has more flexible property and income requirements.

How to Check Eligibility

Before applying for a USDA loan:

  • Use the USDA property eligibility map to verify the address qualifies
  • Check income limits for your county and household size
  • Confirm the property will be your primary residence
  • Verify your credit score meets lender requirements (typically 640+)
