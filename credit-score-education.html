Why Credit Scores Matter

Your credit score is one of the most important factors in determining your mortgage rate and approval. Even small differences in your score can result in thousands of dollars in savings over the life of your loan.

Example: On a $400,000 mortgage, a borrower with a 780 credit score might save over $59,000 in interest compared to someone with a 620 score over a 30-year term.

What Impacts Your Credit Score?

The five factors that determine your FICO score:

  • Payment History (35%): Most important factor. Late payments hurt your score significantly.
  • Credit Utilization (30%): How much credit you're using vs. your total available credit. Keep below 30%.
  • Length of Credit History (15%): Older accounts help your score. Don't close old accounts.
  • New Credit (10%): Recent credit inquiries and new accounts. Too many hurt your score.
  • Credit Mix (10%): Different types of credit (cards, auto loans, mortgages) can help.

DO NOT Do These Things During Your Mortgage Process!

CRITICAL: Your lender will check your credit again before closing!

Even small credit changes can delay or cancel your loan. Avoid these mistakes:

  • DO NOT open new credit cards or accounts
  • DO NOT make large purchases on credit (furniture, cars, appliances)
  • DO NOT co-sign for anyone else's loan
  • DO NOT max out existing credit cards
  • DO NOT close old credit card accounts
  • DO NOT change jobs or become self-employed
  • DO NOT miss any payments on existing debts
  • DO NOT apply for new credit or financing of any kind
  • DO NOT let anyone pull your credit

How to Improve Your Credit Score

Short-term improvements (30-90 days):

  • Pay down credit card balances to below 30% utilization
  • Pay down balances to below 10% for maximum impact
  • Become an authorized user on someone else's good account
  • Dispute any errors on your credit report
  • Ask for higher credit limits (without hard inquiry if possible)

Long-term improvements (6-12 months):

  • Set up automatic payments to never miss a due date
  • Pay bills on time, every time (35% of your score)
  • Keep old accounts open (even if unused)
  • Avoid applying for new credit
  • Diversify credit mix if you only have one type
  • Work with a credit counselor if needed

When to Check Your Credit

DO check your credit when:

  • Planning to buy a home 6-12 months out
  • Using free services like AnnualCreditReport.com
  • Reviewing for errors or fraud
  • Monitoring your score monthly (soft inquiry)

DO NOT check when:

  • Applying for credit cards "just to see" if you'll be approved
  • Shopping for multiple loans without a plan
  • During your active mortgage process (your lender will handle this)

Recent Credit Score Changes

Important update: Fannie Mae eliminated its minimum 620 credit score requirement in November 2025. However, this doesn't mean all borrowers below 620 will be approved. Instead, lenders now look at a broader range of factors including:

  • Borrower reserves and liquid assets
  • Debt-to-income ratio
  • Property characteristics
  • Loan purpose and down payment
  • Payment history on rent, utilities, and other bills

New credit models (VantageScore 4.0, FICO 10T) now include alternative data like rent and utility payments, which may help borrowers with limited traditional credit history.

Free Credit Resources

  • AnnualCreditReport.com: Free credit reports from all 3 bureaus (official site)
  • Experian.com: Free FICO score monitoring
  • Credit Karma: Free VantageScore monitoring
  • Your Credit Card: Many cards now offer free FICO scores

Remember: Mortgage lenders use specific FICO scores (FICO 2, 4, 5) that may differ from scores you see on free apps, but monitoring any score helps you track trends.

Bottom Line

Your credit score significantly impacts your ability to buy a home and the rate you'll pay. Small improvements can save thousands of dollars. Start monitoring and improving your credit 6-12 months before you plan to buy, and avoid common mistakes during your mortgage process.
