Reverse Mortgages (HECM)

Home Equity Conversion Mortgage for Seniors 62+

What is a Reverse Mortgage (HECM)?

A Home Equity Conversion Mortgage (HECM) is an FHA-insured reverse mortgage that allows homeowners aged 62 and older to convert a portion of their home equity into cash without making monthly mortgage payments. You retain ownership of your home and continue living there as long as you meet loan obligations.

Key Requirements (2026)

Minimum Age
62 years old (at least one borrower)
Home Ownership
Own home outright or have low mortgage balance
Occupancy
Must be primary residence
Maximum Claim Amount (2026)
$1,249,125 (used for calculation)

How It Works

Instead of making monthly payments to a lender, the lender makes payments to you based on your home equity. The loan balance grows over time as interest accrues. You never owe more than your home's value, and the loan becomes due when:

  • You permanently move out of the home
  • You sell the property
  • The last borrower passes away
  • You fail to pay property taxes, insurance, or maintain the home

Eligibility Requirements

To qualify for a HECM reverse mortgage, you must:

  • Be at least 62 years old (all borrowers)
  • Own the home outright or have a low mortgage balance that can be paid off at closing
  • Live in the home as your primary residence
  • Maintain the property in good condition
  • Stay current on property taxes and homeowners insurance
  • Have no delinquent federal debt
  • Complete HUD-approved counseling session
  • Meet financial assessment requirements (sufficient income/credit)

Eligible Property Types

  • Single-family homes (1-4 units, must occupy one unit)
  • FHA-approved condominiums
  • Manufactured homes (built after June 1976, meets HUD standards)
  • Townhouses
  • Properties with ADUs (accessory dwelling units) now explicitly allowed

Costs & Fees

Upfront Costs:

  • FHA Mortgage Insurance Premium: 2% of home value (can be financed)
  • Origination Fee: Typically $2,500-$6,000 (can be financed)
  • Closing Costs: Appraisal, title insurance, recording fees (can be financed)

Ongoing Costs:

  • Annual Mortgage Insurance: 0.5% of loan balance
  • Interest: Accrues monthly on outstanding balance

Payout Options

You can receive your funds in several ways:

  • Lump Sum: One-time payment at closing (fixed rate only)
  • Monthly Payments: Fixed amount for set term or for life (tenure)
  • Line of Credit: Draw funds as needed; unused portion grows over time
  • Combination: Mix of lump sum, monthly payments, and/or line of credit

How Much Can You Receive?

The amount depends on:

  • Age of youngest borrower: Older = higher percentage
  • Home value: Up to $1,249,125 maximum claim amount
  • Current interest rates: Lower rates = more funds available
  • Principal Limit Factors (PLFs): Set by HUD based on age

Example: A 70-year-old with a home worth $400,000 might access approximately 50-60% of the home's value, depending on current rates.

Major Benefits

  • No monthly mortgage payments required (taxes/insurance still due)
  • Remain in your home and retain ownership
  • Never owe more than home's value (FHA insurance protects you)
  • Tax-free proceeds (consult tax advisor)
  • Unused line of credit grows over time
  • Can make voluntary payments without penalty
  • Spouse protections available for non-borrowing spouses

Important Considerations

⚠️ Important to Understand:
  • Loan balance increases over time as interest accrues
  • May reduce inheritance for heirs
  • Must continue paying property taxes, insurance, HOA fees, and maintenance
  • If you move to assisted living permanently, loan becomes due
  • Selling costs and loan payoff may leave less equity than expected

Who Should Consider a Reverse Mortgage?

Reverse mortgages work best for homeowners who:

  • Are 62 or older and plan to stay in the home long-term
  • Have significant home equity
  • Need additional retirement income or cash flow
  • Want to eliminate monthly mortgage payments
  • Have no plans to leave home to heirs
  • Cannot qualify for traditional refinancing options

HECM for Purchase

You can use a HECM to purchase a new primary residence! This allows you to buy a home without monthly mortgage payments. Requirements:

  • Substantial down payment required (typically 40-60% of purchase price)
  • Must move in within 60 days
  • All standard HECM requirements apply
  • Single closing process

Required Counseling

Before obtaining a HECM, you must complete a counseling session with a HUD-approved counselor. This ensures you understand:

  • How reverse mortgages work
  • Costs and obligations
  • Alternatives to consider
  • Impact on you and your heirs
